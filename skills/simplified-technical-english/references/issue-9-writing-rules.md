## Section 1 - Words

Source: printed page 1-1-1, PDF page 45.

- Rules 1.1 through 1.4: Use approved words, meanings, parts of speech, and word forms.
- Rules 1.5 through 1.11: Permit necessary technical nouns, but use approved domain terms. Keep each term short, clear, and consistent. Do not use jargon or use a technical noun as a verb.
- Rules 1.12 and 1.13: Permit technical verbs in defined categories. Do not use a technical verb as a noun.
- Rule 1.14: Use American English spelling unless an official directive requires a different spelling.

## Section 2 - Multi-word nouns

Source: printed pages 1-2-1 through 1-2-3, PDF pages 63 through 65.

- Rule 2.1: Keep a multi-word noun to three words or fewer.
- Rule 2.2: When an established technical noun has more than three words, write the full term first. Then introduce a clear short form or use hyphens to show words that form one unit.

Do not shorten an established term in a way that changes its meaning.

## Section 3 - Verbs

Source: printed pages 1-3-1 through 1-3-9, PDF pages 67 through 75.

- Rule 3.1: Use only verb forms that the dictionary permits.
- Rule 3.2: Use the infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, or an approved past participle used as an adjective.
- Rule 3.3: Use a past participle as an adjective only when it describes a condition.
- Rule 3.4: Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions.
- Rule 3.5: Use an `-ing` form only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun.
- Rule 3.6: Use active voice. In a description, use passive voice only when the agent is unknown.
- Rule 3.7: Use an approved verb to describe an action. Do not hide the action in a noun or another part of speech.

## Section 4 - Sentences

Source: printed pages 1-4-1 through 1-4-9, PDF pages 77 through 85.

- Rule 4.1: Write short, clear sentences.
- Rule 4.2: Do not omit necessary words or use contractions.
- Rule 4.3: Use a vertical list for complex text.
- Rule 4.4: Use approved connecting words and phrases to connect related topics.
- Rule 4.5: Use an article or demonstrative adjective before a noun when it is applicable.

## Section 5 - Procedural writing

Source: printed pages 1-5-1 through 1-5-7, PDF pages 87 through 93.

- Rule 5.1: Use a maximum of 20 words in each procedure sentence. The limit also applies to warnings, cautions, and other safety instructions.
- Rule 5.2: Put one instruction in each sentence. Combine actions only when they occur at the same time or when a result follows immediately.
- Rule 5.3: Write an instruction in the imperative form.
- Rule 5.4: Put a condition before the command and separate the condition from the command with a comma.
- Rule 5.5: Use a note only for information. Do not put an instruction, requirement, limit, result, or safety instruction in a note. A note follows the descriptive-writing limit of 25 words per sentence.

## Section 6 - Descriptive writing

Source: printed pages 1-6-1 through 1-6-7, PDF pages 95 through 101.

- Rule 6.1: Give information gradually. Put one subject in each sentence.
- Rule 6.2: Repeat key words and key phrases to show the logical structure.
- Rule 6.3: Use a maximum of 25 words in each sentence.
- Rule 6.4: Use paragraphs to group related information.
- Rule 6.5: Put one topic in each paragraph.
- Rule 6.6: Use no more than six sentences in a paragraph.

Descriptions give information. Do not use an imperative verb in descriptive text.

## Section 7 - Safety instructions

Source: printed pages 1-7-1 through 1-7-3, PDF pages 103 through 105.

- Rule 7.1: Use a label that identifies the risk level. Issue 9 defines a warning as a risk of injury or death and a caution as a risk of damage to objects.
- Rule 7.2: Start with a clear and accurate command or condition.
- Rule 7.3: Explain the risk or possible result.

Use the risk labels and safety conventions that the applicable project, industry, or regulation requires.

## Section 8 - Punctuation and word count

Source: printed pages 1-8-1 through 1-8-7, PDF pages 107 through 113.

- Rule 8.1: Use standard English punctuation, but do not use a semicolon.
- Rule 8.2: Use hyphens to connect words that are directly related.
- Rule 8.3: Use parentheses only for the listed reference, identifier, abbreviation, explanation, plural, or alternative functions.
- Rule 8.4: In a vertical list, a colon ends the sentence for word-count purposes.
- Rule 8.5: Text in parentheses counts as one word in its sentence.
- Rule 8.6: Count a number, number with a unit, abbreviation, alphanumeric identifier, quoted text, title, label, or proper noun as one word.
- Rule 8.7: Count a hyphenated term as one word.

## Section 9 - Writing practices

Source: printed pages 1-9-1 through 1-9-13, PDF pages 115 through 127.

- Rule 9.1: Change the sentence construction when a word-for-word replacement is not sufficient.
- Rule 9.2: Use an approved word only with its approved meaning and part of speech.
- Rule 9.3: Do not combine approved words to make an unapproved phrasal verb.
- Rule 9.4: Use the same terminology and sentence pattern for the same object or action.

## General recommendations

Issue 9 labels GR-1 through GR-8 as recommendations, not STE rules. Source: printed pages 1-9-9 through 1-9-13, PDF pages 123 through 127.

- GR-1: Use `that` when it makes the boundary of a subordinate clause clear.
- GR-2: Recheck each use of `with` because the word can make an instrument, association, or condition ambiguous.
- GR-3 and GR-4: Replace a pronoun when it can refer to more than one noun. Give a clear noun before `this`.
- GR-5: Check false friends when a word resembles a word in another language.
- GR-6: Prefer full English phrases to Latin abbreviations.
- GR-7: Use inclusive, gender-neutral language.
- GR-8: Use the possessive form only when it is clear to the intended reader.

## Review boundary

A short review against this summary is not a full compliance review. For a compliance claim:

1. Classify each text span as procedural or descriptive.
2. Review every applicable rule in the source PDF.
3. Check each non-technical word against the Issue 9 dictionary.
4. Check each technical noun and technical verb against the project's approved terminology.
5. Recheck the final text after all edits.

Do not claim certification or ASD endorsement.
